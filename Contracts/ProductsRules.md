# Products Rules

What the school sells, how it is named, how it is priced, and how stock is counted.

Everything on this page is a rule the school set. Someone at the school can confirm or deny every
line without knowing anything about the system. The decisions *we* took building it are in
`ProductsDesign.md`; the wire format is `ProductsContract.md`.

---

## A product, a version, a size

Three different things, and the school treats them differently.

| | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Producto** | The thing the school names and keeps for years | *Polo buzo* |
| **Versión** | The garment as it was last redesigned | *Polo buzo 2023* |
| **Talla** | The size a parent actually buys | *M* |

**A redesign does not replace the previous garment overnight.** When a new cut of the buzo arrives,
the old one keeps selling until its stock runs out, at its own price. Both are on sale in the same
year, and the school needs to know which of the two a parent bought — the two garments do not look
the same and are not worth the same. In 2023 the school sold four different shorts side by side.

**A version that has left the shelves still has a sales history.** The school reports on what it
sold under an old cut for years afterwards, so that history is never lost when the cut stops being
offered.

## Books vary by grade, not by size

A book is the same book in every size, and a different book in every grade. A book also names its
**editorial**, and a change of editorial is a new version: the 2020 English books came from a
different publisher than the 2019 ones, and they were a different product to buy.

Everything else — an agenda, a plan lector, cloth by the metre — has neither sizes nor grades. It is
just itself.

## Sizes are a fixed list

Garments run `2 4 6 8 10 12 14 16` and then `XS S M L XL XXL`; socks run by age (`2-5 años`,
`6-9 años`, `9-12 años`); ties, ribbons and hats have no size at all. Nobody invents a new size on a
form.

The list has an inherent order, and that order carries a price fact: **the bigger the size, the
higher the price.**

## Price belongs to a size and to a school year

Two prices for the same garment in the same year are normal — a shirt in size 8 costs less than the
same shirt in XL. The school usually sets one price for a run of consecutive sizes and raises it a
step or two along the ladder, but it may price every size separately, and sometimes does.

Every year gets its own prices, and last year's are kept. A product with no price for a given year
is simply not on sale that year.

## Stock is counted per year, and does not carry over

Each school year starts from a **fresh physical count** plus whatever was ordered for the season.
Last year's closing figure is not this year's opening figure, and nobody expects it to be.

## Stock is counted, never typed

The stock figure must always be explainable. It changes only by recording what physically happened,
and every one of those things is a document somebody signed off:

| Movement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inventario inicial | the physical count taken when the year opened |
| Ingreso / Compra | goods received from a supplier; always adds |
| Venta | a sale; reduces stock |
| Devolución / Cambio | a garment handed back; it goes onto the shelf again |

**Nobody types a correction.** There is no "ajuste" — the school can always answer *"why does it say
84?"* by naming the count, the purchases and the sales behind the number. A figure that disagrees
with the shelf means one of those documents is wrong, and the fix is to correct that document, or to
take the count again.

**A return puts the garment back.** When a parent exchanges a size 12 blouse for a size 14, the
school has one more size 12 to sell and one fewer size 14. Recording only the money means the shelf
count drifts a little further from the truth every year.

## A uniform is a set of parts

The school sells a uniform as several separate garments, but it thinks of it as one thing a student
needs — a *uniforme de diario* for a boy in Primaria is a shirt, a trouser and a jumper; the *buzo*
is a polo, a short, a trouser and a jacket.

A set lists **which garments**, and how many of each; some parts are optional. It does **not** fix
the sizes: the size of each part is chosen for the student when the uniform is actually sold, because
no two children take the same size in every garment.

A set may carry its own price for a school year. When it does not, it costs the sum of its parts —
it is a checklist, not a discount.

## Who buys

The counter sells to a **student**, to a **guardian**, or to a **member of staff**. Every sale is in
somebody's name — the school does not sell to an unnamed member of the public, because what it sells
is uniforms and books for its own pupils.

## A sale is paid when it is made

Nothing leaves the counter unpaid. There is no account a family runs up at the shop.

## Returns and exchanges

A garment can be handed back, on its own or swapped for another. The two are the same transaction
with a different balance:

- swapped for something **dearer** — the parent pays the difference;
- swapped for something **cheaper**, or handed back with nothing in return — the school refunds the
  difference;
- swapped for something of the **same price** — no money changes hands at all.

What comes back is credited at **what the parent actually paid for it**, not at this year's price.
Nothing can be handed back twice, and nothing more than was bought.

## The school buys on credit

Goods arrive from a supplier against the supplier's own factura or boleta, and the school pays it
later. From the moment the goods arrive the school **owes** that supplier, and that debt is settled
by its own payment — sometimes covering several invoices at once, oldest first.
